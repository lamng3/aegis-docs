What this is

A recording of the library, not a simulation. experiments/walkthrough/trace.py runs Aegis's own impact ranking, greedy search, UCT, beam search, and best-response locking on the materials ontology of the playground, and writes every step down; this page replays it. Under the ontology, AFF is the affected area of the state shown, as forensics.affected_area computes it (after Fan et al., SIGMOD 2011): the answers each question lost and gained.

How to read it

  1. Impact. Two questions are asked of the ontology. Each element is taken out in turn and both are asked again: the drop in mean F1 is that element's Δ. The elements with Δ above zero glow. They are the vulnerable area, the part the answers depend on, and what an attacker picks first. The AFF line shows which answers each removal changes.
  2. Greedy. Add whichever unit raises harm most, until nothing does. It stops early, because each edit that matters here looks weak until the other has been made.
  3. Bandit. The tree grows downward from "no edits". Each card is a first edit, with its visits and mean harm; the dots under it are the second edits tried after it. Red marks the path being walked, green the chain just opened, orange the rollout, and gold the best chain so far. Below the tree, the root's arms show the bandit's choice: each arm's UCB is its mean harm (solid) plus an exploration bonus that shrinks as the arm is visited (striped), and the highest UCB wins unless the root still has room to open a new arm. An arm whose every chain has been scored is marked explored and is no longer chosen.
  4. Lock. The defender has room for two locks. Each round takes the attacker's best chain, tries locking each triple it removes, and keeps the one that leaves the attacker the least harm.
  5. Ontological match. Ontological graph keys (Ma et al., "Ontology-based Entity Matching in Attributed Graphs", PVLDB 12(10), 2019) match a pattern node labelled l against every node whose label is within distance α of l in the ontology: the relevant set lsim(l). The two worst chains are replayed against that matching. Rewiring Metal under MeltingPoint takes Metal and Alloy out of reach of Material and brings Metal within reach of Temperature, so graph nodes typed Metal stop matching one pattern and start matching another, though the graph was never touched. Aegis does not implement this matching; the trace writes out the paper's definition, with the weights of its Example 3 (0.3 for subClassOf, 0.6 for a descriptive relation) and α = 0.6.
