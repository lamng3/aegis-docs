    Attack search at equal cost

    uv run python experiments/attack_defense/run.py --json results/attack_defense/2026-10-18.json
uv run --with matplotlib python paper/figures.py results/attack_defense/2026-10-18.json

    At equal harm evaluations (beam's count), the most harmful three-action chain each search finds; random and UCT are means of 5 seeds.

    OntologyGreedyBeamRandomUCTEvaluations
    EBSD0.6000.6000.5330.493167
    MatProc0.6191.0000.7760.805119

    UCT beats greedy and beam on XRD at every budget from two actions up (0.763 against 0.644 at four), ties beam at two actions elsewhere, and trails it at three and four where harm is close to additive. Given 200 evaluations it reaches 1.000 on MatProc and 0.945 on mouse anatomy with no spread across seeds; random search still varies by 0.04 to 0.12.

    Protection by locking

    Residual harm of a two-action attacker (the best of beam and UCT) after locking six triples:

    OntologyNo locksRandomTop impactBest response
    EBSD0.4000.3200.4000.133
    MatProc0.8000.4590.0250.025
    XRD0.4000.4000.2120.030
    Mouse anatomy0.7450.6750.0290.019

    Best response is never worse than top impact. On EBSD, six top-impact locks change nothing because the attacker moves to the next query's triples. Ten well-chosen locks remove all of a two-action attacker's harm on every ontology.

    A language-model agent

    Claude Opus 5.5, writing SPARQL against a labeled schema, answers every honest question on all four ontologies and then suffers exactly the template answerer's harm under the worst two-action chain (0.400, 0.800, 0.400, and 0.745). On hierarchy questions the damage is in the ontology, so the cheap template answerer is a faithful proxy during attack search.

    Agent memory

    Over 20 random attacks of one to three forged or removed findings, every attack was detected. Repair by attribution restored every probe and kept all honest work done after the attack; rollback restored the probes and lost that work.
