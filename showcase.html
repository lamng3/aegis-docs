    Replay a real run of the attack search on the small materials ontology, one step at a time: the impact of each element, greedy search, the bandit's search tree, and the locks that defend.

    What this is

    A recording of the library, not a simulation. experiments/walkthrough/trace.py runs Aegis's own impact ranking, greedy search, UCT, beam search, and best-response locking on the materials ontology of the playground, and writes every step down; this page replays it. Under the ontology, AFF is the affected area of the state shown, as forensics.affected_area computes it (after Fan et al., SIGMOD 2011): the answers each question lost and gained.

    How to read it

    1. Impact. Two questions are asked of the ontology. Each element is taken out in turn and both are asked again: the drop in mean F1 is that element's Δ. The elements with Δ above zero glow. They are the vulnerable area, the part the answers depend on, and what an attacker picks first. The AFF line shows which answers each removal changes.
    2. Greedy. Add whichever unit raises harm most, until nothing does. It stops early, because each edit that matters here looks weak until the other has been made.
    3. Bandit. The tree grows downward from "no edits". Each card is a first edit, with its visits and mean harm. The card in play opens a second row: the second edits tried after it, each a card of its own; the others show how many were tried ("+3 next"). Red marks the path being walked, green the chain just opened, orange the rollout, and gold the best chain so far. Below the tree, the root's arms show the bandit's choice: each arm's UCB is its mean harm (solid) plus an exploration bonus that shrinks as the arm is visited (striped), and the highest UCB wins unless the root still has room to open a new arm. An arm whose every chain has been scored is marked explored and is no longer chosen.
    4. Lock. The defender has room for two locks. Each round takes the attacker's best chain, tries locking each triple it removes, and keeps the one that leaves the attacker the least harm.

    The best attack plan. Greedy, the bandit, and locking each end on the plan they leave you with, drawn as a path from the honest ontology through each edit: the harm so far, and the answers that edit changes. The numbers on the ontology mark where each edit lands; step through to watch them land one at a time. After locking, the plan is the attack the defender still faces.

    Keys: ← and → step, space plays. Drag the scrubber to jump to any step.
